State government

State Data Overview

California's state government collects revenue into the General Fund (flexible, mostly income and sales tax) and special funds (earmarked, e.g. gas taxes for roads). Spending also includes bond funds and federal funds.

Sources (all fetched by scripts/fetch_*.py, listed with hashes in data/raw/MANIFEST.csv):

  • Legislative Analyst's Office (LAO) historical revenues, 1950-51 to present, and historical expenditures, 1984-85 to present. These come from Department of Finance and State Controller data.
  • State Controller's Office (SCO) Cities, Counties and Special Districts annual reports (revenues and expenditures, FY2002-03 on), via the SCO's open-data API.
  • BLS CPI-U (via FRED) for inflation, and FRED/Census California population. Census files for county FIPS codes and boundaries.

Caveats

  • The revenue series has a break between 2015-16 and 2016-17: LAO publishes the two periods under different accounting categories, so levels shift slightly. Charts mark the break.
  • 2018-19 personal income and sales tax figures in LAO's file are misaligned; those cells are replaced with DOF Schedule 3 totals (all funds). Every comparison and repair is logged in data/processed/revenue_reconciliation.csv.
  • Local data (SCO) ends at FY2023-24 and excludes K-12 school districts and community colleges, which report to the Department of Education.
  • The newest years (shaded "estimate") are budget estimates and proposals, not closed books.
  • Real dollars use CPI-U (US city average) and the latest complete fiscal year as the base.

Local government

Local Government Revenue

Cities, counties and special districts report finances to the State Controller's Office (SCO). Fiscal years 2002-03 on.

Read this first

  • Not included: K-12 school districts and community colleges report to the Department of Education, not the SCO, so they are not here. Most property tax ultimately funds schools, so these charts understate total local tax collection. County and city tax charts below show what cities and counties themselves receive.
  • Double counting: counties receive large state and federal aid ("intergovernmental"), which also shows up in state spending. Do not add state and local totals without netting this out.
  • Reporting break: SCO changed its category format in FY2016-17, so some groups shift at the dotted line. Internal service funds and fund-type categories (special districts) only exist after the change.
  • Values are as reported by each government (SCO does not audit them), in nominal dollars unless the toggle says otherwise.

Local Taxes

Local Government Spending

By function. Cities and counties report spending in functional groups that SCO redefined in FY2016-17; this page maps both eras onto one set of groups using the finest reported label (see src/ca_taxes/local.py). City utilities, sanitation and transportation moved between categories at the break, so treat jumps at the dotted line as reporting, not policy.

County Maps: Local Taxes and Spending per Person

For each county: taxes collected by the county government plus every city in it, divided by the county's population. San Francisco (a combined city-county) is reported as a city by the SCO and added from there. Excludes school and special districts. Latest year available, nominal dollars.

Total Taxes per Person

State taxes plus city and county taxes, per Californian. This counts taxes California governments *collect*, not what households pay (federal taxes and school-district levies are excluded, and some state tax is paid by businesses and visitors).
